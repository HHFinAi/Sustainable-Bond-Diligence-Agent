# Climate resilience credit and funding — new underwriting overlay

**18 November 2026. Original research method, not financial advice or a new bond recommendation.**

## Core proposition
Climate adaptation can protect collateral and reduce business interruption while simultaneously raising capex needs, debt balances and project-completion risk. The investor owns a **specific debt instrument**, not the macro adaptation spending opportunity. Physical climate hazards do not mechanically translate into stronger insurer profitability or tighter bond spreads.

### Credit transmission matrix
| Obligor | Physical disruption | Adaptation finance | Binding underwriting test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Regulated utility | heat/flood/fire outage, mandated restoration | approved regulated capex, tariff/recovery | regulator's recovery order, balance-sheet bridge, financing cost and capex cadence |
| Municipal / infrastructure | taxable asset loss, service outages | grant + revenue bond, assessed fees | legal recourse, revenues, non-discretionary maintenance and long-duration funding |
| Corporate supplier | plant disruption, supply-chain delay | borrowings or capex to meet funded demand | backlog-to-cash, covenant stress, cash absorption and export/geographic mismatch |
| Insurer/reinsurer | large claims, tail accumulation | reserves, reinsurance and invested assets | net exposures, probable maximum loss scenario, renewal pricing and capital buffer |

**No invented hazard probability, PD/LGD, underwriting-return forecast, quote, coupon or traded bond identifier.** Treat avoided losses as conditional and do not count both lower default risk and a fully credited cash-flow saving without a reconciliation.

## Evidence gates
1. **Obligor** legal name, issue ISIN/CUSIP and currency, paying/guaranteeing entity, maturity and ranking.
2. **Collateral/geography**: actual risk locations, hazard evidence vintage, insured/uninsured losses, deductible, hazard basis, servicing jurisdiction.
3. **Project cash flows**: payer, signed funding authorization, drawdown schedule, matching funds, procurement contract, realistic build time, cost overruns and O&M.
4. **Recovery and downside**: EBITDA/fiscal collection, DSCR, ring-fenced assets, covenants, contingent liabilities, coupon/refinancing and insurance exclusions.
5. **Entry price**: timestamped executable bid/ask, size, settlement, comparables, curve/liquidity sensitivity, funding and tax.
6. **Credit vs label**: separately test creditworthiness, adaptation impact/eligibility and portfolio mandate. A credible use-of-proceeds label does not establish good entry pricing.

## Two bounded case questions
- A hypothetical municipal flood defense: if service revenues do not increase while debt-funded capex and maintenance do, does debt service improve or worsen? Required: audited fiscal accounts, award/fees, legal pledge, post-completion avoided losses, and market quote. **NEEDS_DATA**.
- Utility grid hardening: how do regulated recoveries, storms, capex, receivable delays and interest cost shift free cash flow and net debt/EBITDA? Required: approved rate case, actual network service territory, insurer policy and debt stack. **NEEDS_DATA**.

This overlay applies at the existing **credit, market and challenge stages**, without changing engine route semantics or bypassing existing evidence validation. It is research context only until instrument-specific documents and independent human review exist.
