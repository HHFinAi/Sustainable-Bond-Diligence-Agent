# EIB 3.125% EuGB due 15 May 2037: credible label, unproven entry price

**WATCH | ISIN EU000A3K4EG9 | Information cut: 16 October 2025 | Prepared retrospectively: 3 October 2026**

## Investment judgment

Retain this issue on a sustainable fixed-income research watchlist. The issuer has substantial reported financial buffers, and the bond-specific external review supports accepting the reported allocation process. Neither finding establishes that investors should pay a yield premium. Without issue-specific legal terms and matched historical prices, an entry recommendation would be stronger than the evidence.

For the illustrative mandate below, the proposed entry rule is **no yield concession to a suitably matched EIB conventional bond after incremental costs**, unless an investment committee explicitly values and budgets for the green allocation mandate. That is an analyst policy, not an observed market level. The model shows why: a hypothetical 5 bp yield concession costs approximately 0.497 price points upfront; with the assumed holding-period costs, outperformance needs further relative spread tightening. Strong demand at issuance alone does not supply that thesis.

**Inference:** resources available to absorb losses and fund obligations justify continued credit work. The direction of risk is not uniformly positive: CET1 fell 140 bp, while individually assessed value adjustments rose. Neither ratio proves a near-term payment problem, but both argue against substituting the green label for credit analysis. The loan population is approximately 79% of assets, so credit selection, guarantees, concentrations and correlated borrower stress deserve attention. [S04, p. 104]

**Calculated stress, not a loss forecast:** an additional net loss equal to 1% of the EUR 437.865 billion loan-at-cost population would consume EUR 4.379 billion, or 5.24% of starting own funds; a 3% loss would consume EUR 13.136 billion, or 15.71%. Remaining own funds would be EUR 79.223 billion and EUR 70.465 billion. The stress gives no credit for future earnings, unpaid capital calls or recoveries, and is additional to amounts already recognised. It does not recompute regulatory capital, risk-weighted assets or liquidity under stress. [A06; `credit-stress.csv`]

The principal unresolved credit questions are correlated sovereign/bank/corporate exposure, guarantee enforceability, rising risk-weighted lending, and the liquidity consequences of drawdowns and market disruption. A diversified loan book does not eliminate common European macroeconomic exposure. **Conditional accept for further diligence**, not a completed internal credit rating.

## Green allocation and impact: separate decisions

KPMG's **16 October 2025** bond-specific post-issuance opinion is positive. The reported EuGB-eligible 2024 disbursements are EUR **2,804.30 million**. The report uses the gradual allocation approach; the disclosed allocation excludes nuclear/gas and the Article 5 flexibility pocket. It is an early report covering 2024 assets, not a complete 2025 allocation statement. [S03, pp. 24, 83–85, 102–104]

**Label judgment — accept within that scope.** This is stronger evidence than an announcement or an unreviewed list of eligible sectors. It still requires matching the exact bond and reporting period. The October framework's pre-issuance annex concerns **future issues**; it is not the April bond's original pre-issuance review. The post-issuance document identifies the original factsheet as dated 28 March 2025, but that original was not retrieved. [S03, p. 102 and Annex 2C]

**Allocation judgment — conditional.** Do not divide EUR 2,804.30 million by EUR 3 billion and label the result the final allocation percentage: nominal principal is not verified net proceeds, and the periods differ. The negative EuGB balance in the 2024 report records eligible disbursements before issuance; it is not evidence that bondholders' cash went missing. The ledger needs reconciliation through the first full issue-year report. [S03, p. 24]

**Impact judgment — reject a numerical bond-level impact claim here.** No tonnes of avoided emissions per EUR million are stated in this case. The accessible framework identifies impact reporting, but the underlying project spreadsheet was not retrieved. Even with it, attribution would require the financed share, project baseline, methodology, asset lifetime, and safeguards against double counting. Financing or refinancing an eligible asset is different from establishing that this bond caused additional investment. [S03, p. 25; S10]

The financially material channel is therefore not an assumed coupon uplift from avoided emissions. It is possible demand, liquidity and spread behaviour, plus reputational and mandate risk if allocation claims fail. These channels need market and legal evidence before being valued.

## Valuation and risk: reproducible, conditional calculations

[`calculate.py`](calculate.py) uses only the Python standard library. Each assumed cash flow is in [`cashflows.csv`](cashflows.csv). The base model treats annual yield as one discount rate, with time exponents equal to the 36/365 stub plus complete annual periods:

\[
P(y)=\sum_i CF_i(1+y)^{-t_i},\quad
D_{mod}=\frac{\sum_i t_iCF_i(1+y)^{-t_i-1}}{P},\quad
C=\frac{\sum_i t_i(t_i+1)CF_i(1+y)^{-t_i-2}}{P}.
\]

At the provisional issue-price anchor of 99.627, the assumed schedule gives **3.1630% annual yield**, **9.9377 years modified duration**, **119.0133 years² convexity**, and **EUR 990.07 DV01 per EUR 1 million face**. These are calculated measures, not a verified published yield, current mark or fitted credit spread. [A01–A03; `results.json`]

| Parallel shock at original settlement | Price per 100 | Price change | EUR P&L per EUR 1m face |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Rates −100 bp; spread unchanged | 110.148 | +10.56% | +105,207 |
| Spread +25 bp; rates unchanged | 97.188 | −2.45% | −24,385 |
| Rates +50 bp; spread unchanged | 94.822 | −4.82% | −48,053 |
| Rates +100 bp; spread unchanged | 90.294 | −9.37% | −93,331 |
| Rates +100 bp and spread +50 bp | 86.026 | −13.65% | −136,010 |

The rate/spread split is a scenario label: both enter as an additive yield change. It is not a calibrated yield-curve, key-rate-duration or option-adjusted-spread model. These are immediate price changes, excluding carry, reinvestment, default, bid/offer, taxes and FX. An apparently strong credit can still generate substantial duration losses.

**Contract sensitivity:** moving the assumed first payment to 15 May 2026, with a long first coupon, changes the calibrated yield by approximately **−0.095 bp**. That small numerical difference supports the scale of the risk illustration; it does not resolve which schedule is legally correct. [A02; `results.json`]

## Relative-value break-even and expression

Define hypothetical greenium as **conventional yield minus green yield**; a positive number is a yield concession paid for the green bond. The following identical-cash-flow comparison isolates mechanics. It is **not an observed EIB greenium**.

| Hypothetical greenium | Green price premium per 100 | Further relative tightening needed to cover assumed two-year carry drag and costs |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 bp | 0.000 | 2.01 bp |
| 5 bp | 0.497 | 3.02 bp |
| 10 bp | 0.996 | 4.03 bp |

The approximation is `required additional tightening (yield bp) = (greenium bp × 2 years + 20 bp of value) / 9.9377`. The **20 bp** is an assumed incremental round-trip cost charged to the green position relative to the comparator, not an observed bid/offer. It excludes roll-down and assumes equal rate exposure and constant duration. At a starting 5 bp greenium, the model requires it to grow to roughly 8 bp for the incremental spread gain to offset the assumed drag. Reversion toward zero works against the position. A full holding-period model with actual cash flows, curves and execution costs must replace this approximation before entry. [A04–A05; `relative-value.csv`]

**Expression decision:** maintain the watchlist with no hypothetical holding or position size. If documentation and quotes clear, assess a cash-bond allocation within the portfolio's issuer and duration budgets. If the budget is stated as maximum EUR DV01, allowable face at this historical anchor is approximately `DV01 budget / 990.07 × EUR 1 million`; actual-date risk must be recomputed. No derivative hedge or financing advantage is assumed.
